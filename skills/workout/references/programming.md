# Programming principles

Load this when planning a workout (`prompts/generate.md`). It's the coaching knowledge the generator leans on — general enough to apply to lifting, running, circuits, and mobility, specific enough to make real decisions. Edit it when the *workouts* are wrong; edit a prompt when the *conversation* is wrong.

## The week, not the day

- **Balance patterns across the week:** squat, hinge, push (horizontal and vertical), pull (horizontal and vertical), carry, and conditioning. A neglected pattern gets priority over a favorite one.
- **Don't hit the same pattern hard two days running.** A hard push day is followed by pull, legs, conditioning, or an easy day — not more pushing. Read the last two weeks of the log before choosing.
- **Hard days need easy days around them.** Roughly one hard session per two or three; the rest moderate or easy. Tiredness, poor sleep, or unusual soreness at check-in turns a planned hard day into a moderate one.
- **Fixed days shape the rest.** If they run Tuesdays and Saturdays, legs go on a day that doesn't wreck the run, and the hard lower day isn't the day before the long run.
- **Coming back after a break:** a week or more off means the first session is lighter (about 70% of the last working loads, fewer sets). Say it once, don't apologize for it.

## Progression

- **Progress one variable at a time** on the main movement: load, then reps, then sets, then density (less rest). Small steps — 2.5 to 5% on load, or one or two reps — stick better than big jumps.
- **The debrief decides.** "Solid" or "easy" at the prescribed load → progress next time. "Hard but done" → repeat. "Missed reps" → repeat at 90% or change the rep range. No debrief → repeat.
- **Rep ranges by goal:** strength 3–6 reps, heavier, longer rest (2–3 min); hypertrophy 8–12, moderate, 60–90 s rest; endurance and conditioning 15+, or time-based, short rest. Mixed goals mix ranges across the week, not within one lift.
- **Stalls:** three weeks with no progress on a movement → change the rep range or the variation for two to three weeks, then return. Don't keep grinding the same 5×5.
- **Running progression:** total weekly distance up by roughly 10% at most; one longer run, one quicker session, the rest easy. Pace goals come from a recent timed effort, not from a target.

## Intensity

- **RPE** (rate of perceived exertion, 1–10): RPE 7 leaves about three reps in the tank; RPE 8, two; RPE 9, one; RPE 10, none. Most working sets live at 7–8. Program RPE when no working weight is known, and say "we'll set a baseline today".
- **Caps by check-in:** normal → up to RPE 8 on the main work; tired or sore → RPE 7; "feeling great" and recovered per the log → one movement can go to RPE 9. Max-effort (RPE 10) only for a deliberate benchmark test, not a regular day.
- **Conditioning intensity:** easy means they could talk in full sentences; hard intervals mean they can't. Most conditioning should be easy; hard intervals once a week at most.

## Session structure

- **Warm-up (about 15% of the time):** general movement to raise temperature, then two or three ramp-up sets of the first main movement, light to working weight. Skip nothing here on a tired day; shorten the accessories instead.
- **Main work (about 65%):** one or two movements that serve a goal. This is the part that's protected when time gets cut.
- **Accessories:** two to four movements that balance the week or shore up a weakness. First to go when time is short.
- **Cool-down (5 min):** a couple of stretches for what was trained. Mobility-goal users get more; everyone gets something.
- **Time budget:** a 40-minute session fits a warm-up, two main movements at 3–4 sets, two accessories, and a cool-down. A 20-minute session is a warm-up, one main movement, one accessory or a finisher, done. Don't cram; cut.

## Substitutions

Keep the pattern, change the tool or the range of motion. Common swaps:

| Can't or shouldn't | Because | Use instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Back squat | no rack; low back or knee | goblet squat, split squat, leg press, box squat to a higher box |
| Deadlift from floor | low back; no bar | trap-bar or KB deadlift, RDL, hip thrust, rack pull from higher |
| Overhead press | shoulder | landmine press, incline DB press, high-incline push-up, or skip vertical push entirely |
| Bench press | no bench; shoulder | floor press, push-up variations, DB press with neutral grip |
| Pull-ups | can't yet; no bar | inverted rows, band-assisted pull-ups, lat pulldown, negatives (if 1–2 clean reps exist) |
| Lunges | knee | step-ups to a low box, split squat with shorter range, sled push, glute bridge |
| KB swings | low back; shoulder on the day | suitcase or farmer carry, hip thrust, sled, bike intervals |
| Running | knee, shin, no time | bike, rower, incline walk, swimming, jump rope (if joints allow) |
| Jumping / plyometrics | joints; downstairs neighbors | sled, bike sprints, med-ball throws into a wall, fast step-ups |
| Any loaded movement | only bodyweight available | tempo (3-second lowering), pauses, unilateral versions, higher reps to RPE |

When a limitation removes a whole pattern (no pressing at all this month), say so in the day's first line and rebalance the week around what's available rather than leaving a hole.

## Signals to change the plan

- **Soreness for three days or more** after a session → too much volume; cut sets by a third next time on that pattern.
- **Sleep or energy reported low two check-ins in a row** → an easy week: same movements, fewer sets, RPE 6–7. Say so once.
- **Pain** (sharp, joint, worsening) → stop the movement, record it, substitute; suggest getting it looked at if it persists. Never "work through it".
- **Motivation dipping** in the debriefs → shorter sessions for a week, or a session built entirely from what they like. Consistency beats optimal.
- **A goal met** → celebrate in one line, set the next anchor, adjust the week.
